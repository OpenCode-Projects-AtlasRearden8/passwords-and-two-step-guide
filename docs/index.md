# Start Here

Welcome. If sign-ins, passwords, and "verify it's really you" screens make you
tired, this guide is for you. It's written like a friend talking you through it:
one step at a time, plain English, no shame about whatever you've been doing so
far. Whatever passwords are on your sticky notes right now — you're not behind,
you're just at the beginning of one quiet evening's work.

## The whole idea in four sentences

1. Every important account (email, bank, phone) gets a **unique password** nobody
   else knows — stored in a **password manager** so you don't have to remember them.
2. You remember exactly **one** strong thing: a long passphrase that unlocks the manager.
3. Your most important accounts get a **second lock** (a two-step code or a passkey),
   so even a stolen password isn't enough for a stranger.
4. You keep **backup codes** somewhere you can actually find them, and about once a
   season, you spend one evening tidying up.

That's it. Everything in this book is a friendlier, more detailed version of
those four sentences.

## A few promises this guide makes

- **No product worship.** We'll talk about what a password manager needs to do
  and give familiar examples, not brand religion.
- **No scary math.** You'll never be asked to trust jargon you haven't been
  given a definition for. If a term shows up, it's in the glossary.
- **No impossible standards.** "Good enough, done" beats "perfect, never
  started." The tidy plan is designed for one real evening, not a fantasy week.

## The two tension points, so you're not surprised

- **Switching to a password manager takes a little friction up front** (moving
  passwords in, one learning curve), and afterward it permanently removes
  friction everywhere else. Pay once, save forever.
- **Two-step codes add one small step at sign-in** (glance, type six digits),
  and in exchange they make almost every break-in story structurally
  impossible. That's the best trade on the internet.
